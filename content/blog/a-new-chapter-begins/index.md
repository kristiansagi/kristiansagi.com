Last week I wrapped up my final day at Dynatrace. It was just before the winter holidays, and as I took a moment to reflect on the year and my future, I realized how much has been achieved during my nearly three years with the company.

When I first joined, it was a small team working out of a room in Rotermanni Spaces HUB in Tallinn, focused on integrating and developing something that had the potential to make a big impact. Now, we’re in our own building, with 3000 square meters of office space, fully functioning teams, proper management structures, and our team is three times the size it was back then. We’ve built a strong culture, and we’re one of the highest-rated employers in Tallinn. There’s still a lot to do, and the plans ahead are ambitious.

It would have been easy to continue riding the momentum of what we’ve built. But as I looked deeper, I realized something was missing. At first, I couldn’t quite put my finger on it. Then it hit me. The excitement of building something new, the pace of doing what nobody had done before, had turned into following a set plan and executing what was already decided. While this structure works for some, I’ve always done my best work in the unknown, where you take risks and figure things out as you go. I need to be where I can once again build and make a difference.

> _Steve Jobs once said: “It’s only by saying no that you can concentrate on the things that are really important.”_

And with that, I made the decision to say no to continuing in my current role. I made sure that my team has the best possible path forward without me, and we celebrated what we had built together.

A huge thank you to everyone at Dynatrace, especially my people in the Tallinn Lab: Kati Päike, Raido Karro, Indrek Übius, Dmitri Maksimov, Maxim Pokrovskiy and the rest of the amazing team we have. I also know that without these people, it would have been impossible: Gordon Meyer, Samuel Moser, Elke Oberhuber, Chris Werding, Magdalena Nalepa, Tamás Antal and so many others, whose support made those years so good.

People have asked me countless times what’s next, and to be honest, I don’t know.

> _As the famous Buddhist quote says, “Do not dwell in the past, do not dream of the future, concentrate the mind on the present moment.”_

That’s where I’ll put my energy for now, before I start on the next thing.

_Update, 2026: today I lead [Entringo](https://entringo.com/) as CEO._
